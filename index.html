Who voted how: exit polls

Exit poll results for Florida in 2024, 2020 and 2018 (Edison Research, as published by CNN), plus the 2026 midterms from MS NOW, which is a national poll and not Florida. Each row shows how a group split between the two main parties and how much of the electorate it made up.

How this data is handled

  • Weighting: These are the crosstabs as the pollster published them, and I did not re-weight anything. The 2020 total row (Biden 47.86, Trump 51.22) matches the actual Florida result, which suggests the figures were adjusted to the final vote. I have not seen a methodology statement that confirms it.
  • Sample sizes: CNN lists 3,293 total respondents for the 2024 Florida presidential poll, shown above the table. The 2020 and 2018 tables I could retrieve show only each group's share of voters, so there are no counts for those. Group-level counts are not published in any of them.
  • Florida vs. national: Every poll is marked. 2024, 2020, 2018 and the 2026 Senate and Governor entries are Florida electorates. The "2026 Midterms (House vote)" entry from MS NOW is national, a sample of voters across the U.S. voting for the U.S. House, credited to the Associated Press. Do not read that one as Florida. Neither 2026 source lists a respondent count or methodology.
  • 2026 Florida detail: From NBC's expanded exit poll view on the Senate and Governor pages (data from the Associated Press), 161 breakdowns for the Senate race and 150 for the Governor race. NBC labels the tab "National Exit Polls", but the numbers fit Florida: white voters are 67% of the electorate, the questions name Moody, Nixon, Donalds and Jolly, and the splits work out to about 51% for Moody and 50% for Donalds. The percentages were read from bar chart labels by position in saved PDFs, and every Democratic and Republican pair adds up to about 100, but spot check a few against the live NBC page. No respondent count or methodology is listed.
  • Not included: 2022 (the saved CNN pages were the national House results, not Florida) and the 2024 Florida Senate poll. The 2026 table on Wikipedia is a commented-out placeholder borrowed from another state, so I left it out.
  • Small groups can be marked n/a where the pollster judged the sample too small.

Sources to verify

    • 2026 numbers are unofficial election night results from the state, about 99% of expected votes in, and will move until counties certify.
    • Margin is Democratic share minus Republican share of all votes cast in the county. Shift is the 2026 margin minus the comparison margin, in percentage points. Turnout differs a lot between presidential and midterm years, so shifts against 2024 President read differently than shifts against 2022.
    • The governor races add up slightly differently from their statewide totals. The 2022 county rows sum to 4,614,418 for DeSantis and 3,106,328 for Crist, and the 2018 rows sum to 4,075,736 for DeSantis. Check the state's results archive before quoting exact numbers from those two.
